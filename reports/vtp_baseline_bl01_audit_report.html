Core Structural Finding: Across 35 settlement days (10 BL01, 3 self-nominated VTPs), settled wholesale turn-down of 145,368.0 MWh is accompanied by 59,811.2 MWh of un-notified rebound (41.1% leakage; £6,368,100 implied unsettled compensation) and 0.603 MWh/SP non-active baseline MAE. Rather than indicating individual non-compliance, these signatures reflect rational commercial responses to P415/P376 settlement mechanics: one-sided wholesale notification creates a free option on baseline noise, un-notified overnight EV charging escapes negative compensation, and continuous event-day exclusion freezes BL01 baselines far above current load.

1. Visual Evidence: Baseline vs Actual Metered Demand & Perverse Incentives

How to read these six charts: Each chart below is chosen to isolate one link in the empirical chain connecting P376/P415 settlement rules to observed market outcomes. Together they move from concrete portfolio behaviour to the underlying mathematical mechanism and its market-wide economic impact:

  • Charts 1–3 (Portfolio Archetypes): Chart 1 retains every raw half-hour (1,682 SPs, zero averaging) and a 35×48 gap heatmap for Axle Energy to expose frozen daytime BL01 drift, overnight rebound, and inactive-daytime gaps; Charts 2–3 contrast REFLEXI (un-notified overnight EV rebound) and Joulen (quantised self-nominated SEVs) on a 24-hour qm vs sev scale.
  • Chart 4 (Mechanism): Zooms out to a 35-day daily series for Axle Energy to show how P376's event-day exclusion rule mathematically locks baselines above actual load.
  • Chart 5 (Incentive Test): Tests the "free option" hypothesis statistically by comparing baseline error distributions on notified (wmi > 0) versus un-notified (wmi = 0) Settlement Periods.
  • Chart 6 (Wholesale Impact): Aggregates all active VTPs by System Buy Price (SBP) regime to test whether dispatch aligns with genuine system scarcity relative to Supplier Sourcing Cost (SSC).

Why this chart

That red-shaded surge represents 26,966 MWh of un-notified rebound — 108.1% of its total settled turn-down — leaving suppliers with £2.87M in implied unsettled SSC exposure.

4. P376 BL01 Event-Day Lookback Freeze — Axle Energy (35 Consecutive Days)
Continuous activation exhausts non-event lookback days, keeping settled SEV frozen near ~3,500 MWh/day while actual demand averages ~1,800 MWh/day.
Settled SEV (frozen) Actual metered (qm) Raw rolling BL01
Why this chart

Chart 1 shows that Axle Energy's baseline sits roughly double its actual load, but a 24-hour average cannot prove why it fails to self-correct. This 35-day daily series tests the P376 BL01 event-day exclusion rule directly by comparing settled sev (red) against actual daily demand (black) and an independent rolling BL01 counterfactual computed without event-day exclusion (dashed amber).

What it actually shows

Because P376 excludes prior event days from the 10-working-day / middle-2-of-4-weekend lookback, activating on all 35/35 days prevents recent metered demand from ever entering the calculation. Settled SEV stays locked at a mean of 3,284 MWh/day (even rising from 2,993 MWh on Oct 1 to 3,710 MWh on Nov 4) while actual demand averages 1,531 MWh/day (a 2.15× structural gap). By contrast, the dashed amber rolling BL01 line tracks actual demand closely — visually isolating the exact distortion created by uncapped event-day exclusion.

Settled turn-down (MWh) Un-notified rebound (MWh)
Why this chart

The central economic test for P510 is whether P415 compensation directs demand turn-down toward genuine system scarcity. Grouping all 35 days of market-wide settled turn-down (blue) and un-notified rebound (red) into five System Buy Price (SBP) buckets relative to the ~£106/MWh Supplier Sourcing Cost (SSC) reveals whether fixed retail-referenced payments distort wholesale dispatch.

What it actually shows

Although most turn-down sits in the £106–£180 and >£180/MWh scarcity buckets, 15,688 MWh (10.8%) was still claimed across the three left-hand regimes where SBP < SSC — including 681 MWh during negative-price (< £0) surplus periods when cutting demand is counter-economic. Equally important, red un-notified rebound bars appear in every bucket: 77.8% (46,518 MWh) of unsettled rebound actually occurs during high-price periods (SBP ≥ £106/MWh), contradicting the assumption that rebound is harmlessly confined to cheap overnight hours.

4. Structural Policy Implications for P376 (BL01), Issue 127 & P510

1. Symmetric / Continuous Settlement Exposure

Allowing VTPs to nominate wmi > 0 selectively during daytime hours while leaving overnight EV recharge at wmi = 0 omits 59,811 MWh (£6.37M implied SSC) of rebound load. Requiring symmetric 24-hour energy-neutral settlement (or settling rebound windows automatically for load-shifting portfolios) eliminates the asymmetric free option.

2. Cap P376 BL01 Event-Day Lookback Freezing

When portfolios activate 34–35 consecutive days (e.g. AXLEENER, REFLEXI), BL01's exclusion of prior event days locks baselines onto stale historical profiles (~2× actual daily demand for AXLEENER). Introducing a hard lookback staleness limit, rolling decay, or tariff-aware counterfactuals prevents permanent baseline drift.

3. Mandatory Baseline Methodology Registry (Issue 127)

Self-nominated SEVs (e.g. POWONTEC's 100% 2dp quantised values and 0 MW off-peak drops) and sign-inverted test SEVs (COPPER) are indistinguishable to suppliers receiving S0141/P0287 flows. Publishing each SBMU's approved baseline methodology and sevij audit trail restores market transparency.

4. Align Compensation with Wholesale Scarcity (P510)

15,688 MWh (10.8%) of settled turn-down occurred when SBP < SSC. Replacing fixed retail-referenced SSC payments with dynamic wholesale-linked settlement removes incentives to claim turn-down during surplus periods.
